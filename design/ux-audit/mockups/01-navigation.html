Why these four

  1. Search becomes a tab. It is a weekly job and the engine is a strength (chips, suggestions), but today the entry point is a 36 px icon. A tab also gives recent searches a home.
  2. Settings becomes a tab. People expect to find Settings without hunting, and a gear on another screen isn't obvious. Settings starts with Backup, so the safety details are one tap away from both Photos (the chip) and Settings.
  3. Devices and Storage fold into Backup. People open Devices to answer "is it backed up?". The Backup page answers that first and lists devices below. The daily answer is already on Photos, so Backup doesn't need a tab of its own.
  4. No avatar menu. Everything in it gets a real place: Favorites goes to Albums; Storage and Devices go to Settings → Backup; Settings gets its own tab; Recently Deleted goes to the bottom of Albums and to Photos ⋯.
  5. One icon set. Monochrome outline icons (Material Symbols on Android, SF Symbols-style on the Mac) replace the mix of emoji and glyphs (F20).
  6. Material 3 nav bar: an active pill indicator, labels always shown, 80 dp tall with a 48 dp target per item.

Desktop: sidebar by frequency

Today's sidebar is a flat list: Library, Favorites, People, Albums, Shared, Devices, Storage, Recently Deleted, Settings. The redesign groups it by job. Library holds what you browse. Albums holds the albums themselves, so one click opens one; shared albums have a badge. Backup is a single status row that tells you its state without a click. Settings sits at the bottom, as in macOS apps.
